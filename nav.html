How the numbers are made

Terrain comes from the LOLA 80 m south-pole elevation model (NASA GSFC). From each site we cast 2,880 rays out to 300 km over the curved Moon and keep the highest terrain in every quarter degree of azimuth. That outline is the horizon you see in the panorama.

Sun and Earth positions come from NAIF SPICE (DE421) in the Moon-fixed frame, hour by hour, so libration and the lunar seasons are included. The Sun counts as up by the share of its disc clear of the terrain, the Earth by its centre.

The lander is an hourly energy balance: Sun-tracking panels, a battery, a working load and a sleep mode that keeps the heaters on. Research and education prototype, not flight certification. Earth visibility is geometric, not radio link quality.
